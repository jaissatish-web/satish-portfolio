How to perform a 5-point calibration

A practical field workflow for checking a linear 4–20 mA pressure transmitter.

A five-point calibration checks the transmitter at 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of its configured range. A proper test applies points in both rising and falling directions so you can observe linearity and hysteresis.

Before starting

  • Confirm the tag number, calibrated range, engineering unit, and accuracy requirement.
  • Review the approved data sheet, calibration procedure, and loop diagram.
  • Use suitable, in-date test equipment with adequate accuracy.
  • Isolate the process connection and make the instrument safe according to the permit and isolation procedure.
Never apply a test pressure beyond the transmitter or connected equipment's allowable limit.

Calculate the test points

For a transmitter ranged 0–10 bar, the standard applied values and ideal outputs are:

PointApplied pressureIdeal output
0%0 bar4 mA
25%2.5 bar8 mA
50%5 bar12 mA
75%7.5 bar16 mA
100%10 bar20 mA

Generate your calibration points

Run the test

  1. Connect the pressure source and reference standard without leaks.
  2. Connect a loop calibrator or precision meter to measure the output current.
  3. Apply each rising point and allow the reading to stabilize.
  4. Record applied pressure, expected output, actual output, and error.
  5. After 100%, repeat the readings in the falling direction.
  6. If results exceed the approved tolerance, adjust zero and span or perform sensor trim according to the manufacturer procedure.
  7. Repeat the full as-left test and record the final results.

Close the work correctly

Restore process connections, check for leaks, remove test equipment, return the loop to service, verify the control-room indication, and complete the calibration record or ITR with the equipment details and as-found/as-left results.
