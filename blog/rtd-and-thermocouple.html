Pt100 and thermocouples: two different conversion problems

Compare resistance measurement with thermocouple EMF and reference-junction compensation.

By Satish Kumar Jaiswal · 2026-09-23

RTD Pt100 CalculatorThermocouple Converter

A Pt100 is a resistance sensor. A thermocouple produces a small thermoelectric voltage related to its junction temperatures and thermocouple type. The two sensors need different input circuits and different conversion methods. Choosing a temperature unit alone is not enough to configure either measurement.

Pt100: start with the element resistance

The nominal Pt100 used by this tool is 100 Ω at 0 °C. The Callendar–Van Dusen relationship describes its nonlinear resistance curve. The tool uses the nominal 0.00385 platinum characteristic between −200 and 850 °C; the actual sensor assembly may have a much narrower service range.

Worked example

At 100 °C, nominal element resistance is 138.5055 Ω. In a two-wire circuit with two 1 Ω leads, the measured resistance is 140.5055 Ω. Ignoring those leads creates an apparent temperature increase.

Three-wire compensation assumes suitable matched leads and a compatible measuring circuit. Four-wire sensing reduces the influence of lead resistance. The tool models ideal compensation for those modes; it does not predict wiring imbalance or self-heating.

Thermocouples: the reference junction matters

NIST reference functions relate temperature and EMF for a reference junction at 0 °C. For a nonzero reference, subtract the reference-junction EMF when calculating measured voltage. To recover hot-junction temperature, add it back before inverting the characteristic.

Try Type K

At a 100 °C hot junction and a 0 °C reference, the output is about 4.096 mV. Change only the reference to 25 °C and observe that the measured voltage decreases while hot-junction temperature stays unchanged.

Supported scope

This converter supports positive-temperature branches for K (0–1372 °C), J (0–760 °C) and T (0–400 °C), with a 0–100 °C reference junction. It rejects unsupported values. Correct polarity, extension-cable type and terminal conditions still matter in a real installation.
