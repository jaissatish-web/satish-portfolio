Calibration error: percent of span is not percent of reading

Calculate signed error and interpret a tolerance comparison correctly.

By Satish Kumar Jaiswal · 2026-09-23

Calibration Error5-Point Calibration

A calibration comparison starts with two values: the reference or expected value and the instrument’s measured value. Their difference is the signed error. Before expressing it as a percentage, identify the denominator specified by the instrument or procedure.

Worked example

A 0–100 bar instrument reads 50.2 bar when the reference is 50.0 bar. Error is +0.2 bar. As a percentage of span it is +0.2%; as a percentage of the 50 bar reading it is +0.4%. Those percentages describe different requirements.

Compare like with like

The tool accepts a tolerance expressed as percent of span. With an entered tolerance of ±0.25% of span, the example receives a basic pass result. The chart places the measured error against the positive and negative limits. A negative error means the instrument reads below the expected value.

One point cannot describe the whole range

Check the points required by the procedure. A satisfactory midpoint does not prove that zero, upper range or intermediate values are satisfactory. Rising and falling sequences can reveal different readings at the same applied point; retain their direction when recording results.

Uncertainty and decision rules matter

The displayed pass/fail result is only a numerical comparison with the tolerance you enter. It does not include reference uncertainty, repeatability, environmental contributions or guard bands. Apply the project’s agreed decision rule before issuing a conformity statement.

Keep the original evidence

Record as-found values before adjustment. If an adjustment is authorized, record as-left values separately with the reference equipment identification and applicable procedure revision. Avoid replacing the original result with only a final “pass” label: the numeric evidence is what supports future review and drift analysis.
