Loop checks, FAT and SAT: make the evidence traceable

Use distinct test records, clear acceptance criteria and honest progress indicators.

By Satish Kumar Jaiswal · 2026-09-23

Loop Check ChecklistFAT / SAT Checklist

A useful commissioning record tells the next engineer what was checked, against which requirement, what happened and who reviewed it. A percentage-complete figure is helpful for planning, but it cannot replace those details.

Keep the scope clear

A loop check follows the defined signal path and verifies applicable field and receiving-system behavior. A factory acceptance test examines the agreed factory scope before shipment. A site acceptance test addresses the installed system and agreed site scope. Their boundaries depend on the project contract and approved procedures.

Give each record a unique identity

Enter an instrument tag or test ID, drawing or procedure reference and reviewer. For a new tag, the browser loads a separate record. The checklist stores progress only on the device and browser used; it is not a shared commissioning database or an electronic signature system.

Example record structure

Use “PT-101-loop” for one loop and “DCS-panel-A-SAT” for an agreed system test. Record the approved revision, actual readings and deviations in the notes. Export each record before handing it over or changing devices.

Distinguish review from acceptance

Pass, fail and not applicable all represent reviewed items, so they contribute to reviewed progress. Pending items remain unreviewed. A record with all items reviewed can still contain failed checks. Resolve or formally disposition those failures through the project process.

Close the test deliberately

  1. Compare observations with the approved acceptance criteria.
  2. Record outstanding punch items and their references.
  3. Confirm restoration of the tested configuration where required.
  4. Export and retain the results with supporting evidence.
  5. Obtain the required witness and acceptance signatures outside this local checklist.

The supplied items are a starting aid. They do not replace the project ITR, approved procedure or equipment-specific instructions.
