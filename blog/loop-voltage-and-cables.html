Will the transmitter have enough voltage?

Build a loop voltage budget and understand the contribution of cable resistance.

By Satish Kumar Jaiswal · 2026-09-23

Loop Voltage BudgetCable Voltage DropOhm’s Law & Power

A loop may behave normally at low current and become unreliable near its upper range if the supply cannot cover all voltage requirements. A useful first check is to account for the transmitter minimum terminal voltage, resistive losses and any additional fixed drops.

Worked budget

Start with a minimum 24 V supply. A 250 Ω resistance drops 5 V at 20 mA. If the transmitter needs 12 V and other devices drop 2 V, the remaining margin is 24 − 5 − 12 − 2 = 5 V.

Use the complete current path

For a two-conductor cable, current travels to the device and back. Enter one-way route length in the cable calculator; it includes the return path. At 20 °C, 500 m of one-way copper route with 1.5 mm² conductors gives an estimated loop resistance of 11.494 Ω and a 0.2299 V drop at 20 mA.

Manufacturer cable data may give a different maximum resistance because of construction and tolerance. Use that data for a final budget. The visual curve shows how estimated voltage drop increases with route length while the other inputs remain fixed.

Choose the real worst case

Use the minimum supply voltage expected at the circuit, the maximum configured loop current and applicable device data. Include receiver resistance, terminals, cable and barriers as appropriate, without counting the same loss twice. Change the current and resistance to see which part of the budget consumes the available margin.

What the calculation does not approve

A positive margin is only a voltage check. It does not establish intrinsic-safety compatibility, HART communication performance, conductor ampacity, protective-device coordination or an installation approval. The cable tool is a DC voltage-drop estimate, not a complete cable-sizing package.

For a quick cross-check, Ohm’s law uses amperes: 20 mA is 0.020 A. Confusing those units makes the calculated drop one thousand times too large.
